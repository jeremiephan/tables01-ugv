Starburst Tables 01 accompanies our June 2026 UGV white paper. It is not a database in the sense of Pitchbook, CB Insights, or Dealroom — those products give you ground truth on companies. Thesis

The civil-vs-defense binary is analytically dead. Five superposed markets, one capital flow.

Capital flows still follow strict bipolarity (defense funds, defense LPs), but technical attributes — autonomy stack, sensor sophistication, cadence — span a continuum. Operators that look "civil" by funding label deploy AI stacks indistinguishable from "defense" peers; operators labelled "defense" use commodity components from civil supply chains. Declared dual-use ≠ real dual-use.

Tables 01 maps this continuum across five superposed markets — Logistics · Combat AP · EOD/Route Clearance · ISR · Civil-defense — plus a sixth Convergence layer where strategic partnerships redistribute the pieces. The framework is reproducible: Tables 02 (drones, Q4 2026) and Tables 03 (autonomous propulsion, Q2 2027) will use the same grid.

Tables 01 · UGV · Methodology

How we drew the line.

Tables 01 is a thinking instrument, not a database. This page documents what we know, what we don't, and the choices we made when the evidence was thin.

Why this exists

The European UGV market does not behave as a single market. Five distinct markets coexist: Logistics support, Combat anti-personnel, EOD-Route Clearance, Recon ground, and Civil-defense dual-use. An operator can hold a primary position on one market and a secondary position on another. Capital and combat-validation flow through these markets unevenly.

Existing data products (Pitchbook, CB Insights, Dealroom) treat operators as point entities — this company raised X, employs Y, deployed Z. They are right about ground truth on companies. They miss the structural reading: which market does this operator dominate, where is it secondary, and how does its position change when (for example) Rheinmetall acquires DOK-ING?

Tables 01 is one structural reading among others. We make our reading explicit so it can be argued against.

What's in the dataset

39 active operators, tracked as of April 2026. To enter the dataset, an operator must:

  • Hold an active UGV product line (terrestrial unmanned vehicle) in 2024-2026, with public evidence of operational use, deployed prototype, or signed customer contract.
  • Be headquartered in a Western country (NATO + Western-aligned + Ukraine). The dataset includes UA, DE, USA, FR, IL, EE, FI, LV, PL, CZ, HR, IT, ES, UK.
  • Have verifiable public sources for at least the actor's name, country, and one product line. Tier C-only entries are flagged.

The dataset is biased toward operators with sufficient public footprint. Confidential or stealth-mode operators are deliberately excluded — Tables 01 cannot reason about what it cannot source.

What's NOT in the dataset (and why)

Several operators that appear in adjacent maps are absent here. The exclusion criteria:

Helsing Pure AI defense software / aerial autonomy. No UGV product line. Referenced as external node when partnered with ARX (Sept 2025).
Naïo Technologies Civil agriculture only. No defense product line.
ANYbotics, Verity Civil-first robotics with limited dual-use evidence. Watchlist for Tables 02.
Threod, DefSecIntel, Skyeton Primary product line is UAS, not UGV.
Tecdron, Delair, Aut Solutions EU Insufficient public evidence of active UGV deployment 2024-2026.
Frontline (UA) Heavy combat operator but ranked outside top 39 by deployment volume. Referenced as external node in convergence analysis.

Exclusion is not a judgment of value. Several excluded operators may join Tables 02 (UAS) or appear in future revisions if new evidence emerges.

How positions are determined

Each operator has 1-4 positions across the 5 market faces. Each position is encoded as cadence × autonomy stack on a 1-3 scale, with a role flag (P = primary market, S = secondary).

Cadence axis (X)

  • 1 · Artisanal — one-off prototypes, low volume (<10 units/year)
  • 2 · Small batch — repeatable production (10-100 units/year)
  • 3 · Mass-producible — serial production (>100 units/year, supply chain locked)

Autonomy stack axis (Y)

  • 1 · Teleoperated — remote control, no autonomy
  • 2 · Assisted — waypoint navigation, basic perception, supervised autonomy
  • 3 · Mature autonomy — multi-modal autonomy stack, ML/SLAM/teaming, demonstrated unsupervised operation

P / S role

A Primary position is the market where the operator generates the majority of revenue or operational volume. Secondary positions are documented but ancillary. An operator can have multiple Primary positions (e.g., Rheinmetall on F1 + F2 + F3 + F4 post-DOK-ING).

Positions are derived from the same source tier as the actor's record. Where evidence is contested, the operator is flagged. Where evidence is undocumented (Tier C only), the position is held but flagged as such.

Source tiers

  • Tier A — Primary documents: government contracts (DGA, MoD, Pentagon), SEC filings, official corporate announcements, EDF / iMUGS / EDA project documents.
  • Tier B — Reputable journalism: Reuters, Bloomberg, Financial Times, Janes, Defense News, Defense Post, Calibre Defense, EDR Magazine, Forbes, The Economist.
  • Tier C — Company-only sources: corporate websites, company press releases, vendor pitch decks. Marked tc in the dataset.

32 of 39 operators have at least one Tier A or B source. 7 of 39 are documented from Tier C only — these are typically smaller mid-tier emerging operators (LV, ES, some PL/CZ entries).

Validation process

Every position in the dataset is reviewed by Starburst aerospace & defense consultants before publication. Where the analyst position differs from the public source reading, both are documented internally and the public source reading is preferred unless the analyst position is itself sourced.

The dataset is updated quarterly. Major events (acquisitions, JVs above €10M, combat-validation events documented in Tier A/B) trigger interim revisions.

Scoring boost rule

UA pure-play combat operators (DevDroid, Temerland, Roboneers, UkrPrototype, Ratel) are pushed to minimum A-tier on combat validation, even when the underlying scoring formula gives them a lower score. Justification:

  • The formula cadence × deployment × diversity × iteration systematically underweights pure-play combat operators with high combat_factor but low diversity.
  • The asymmetric learning thesis (UA mass-deployed combat is the only operational doctrine in Europe) requires that combat-validated operators are not buried by diversity-weighted scores.
  • This is a declared methodological choice, documented for transparency rather than presented as neutral.

What we don't know

The dataset has known blind spots:

  • Stealth-mode operators (especially USA + IL ecosystem) are absent until they surface publicly.
  • Production volumes for UA operators are estimated within a wide range. Exact unit counts are classified.
  • Autonomy stack maturity is self-declared by most non-USA operators. We rely on demonstration evidence rather than internal capability claims.
  • Civil-defense (F5) market is the least densely populated and may be under-represented.
  • Mid-tier Eastern European operators (PL, CZ, LV) may have additional product lines not yet publicly documented.

When evidence is thin, we mark uncertainty rather than guess. Read Sources for the per-actor source ledger.
